An entry carries no short name, so every list of entries is a list of paragraphs.

The shortest thing an entry holds is its summary. A summary states a claim rather than naming one.
The ones in the graph at `/Users/felix/Codepot/devops/format-d-fdbox` run to several sentences. A
table shows the first few words of each and cuts the rest. A drawn graph has nowhere to put any of
it, so an entry there is a coloured shape a reader hovers over to identify.

## What it costs

A reader scanning a hundred entries reads a hundred cut sentences and recognises none of them again.
Nothing in the viewer can name an entry in a column, in a graph, in a chain of references, or in a
list of what an entry points at. The identifier is the only short handle it has, and `d-tac-uaj` says
nothing about what it decided.

## What sdd does with a name it does not know

sdd reads frontmatter with a plain decoder that ignores keys it has no field for, so an added `title`
would not stop it reading a graph. Every command that rewrites an entry rebuilds the frontmatter from
its own fields alone, so `sdd rewrite`, `sdd summarize` and `sdd lint --fix` each drop such a key
without saying so. Regenerating a summary is ordinary practice, which makes the loss ordinary too.

The viewer cannot write one in any case. It never writes to a repository it reads, and that refusal
lives in the type rather than in a caller's manners.

## What closes it

Three shapes answer this, and the choice between them is open.

- A title derived at read time from the summary, costing nothing and storing nothing, and weaker for it.
- A title the viewer keeps in its own cache, keyed by the entry, and by what it holds. It leaves the
  repository untouched, and gives every machine its own answer.
- A field sdd itself carries. It is the only shape surviving a rewrite, and the only one reaching
  everyone who reads the same graph. It is a change to that tool rather than to this viewer.
