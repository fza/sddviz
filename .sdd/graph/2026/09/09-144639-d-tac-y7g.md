The viewer shows a graph three ways at once, and one selected entry ties them together.

A bar across the top names the open repository and the revision its graph reads at. `Repository`
carries a `Name`, which is the directory the graph lives in. The back end splits it, because a path
belongs to the front end to show and never to take apart.

The table lists every entry and scrolls in its own region. A row selects, and a control on each row
draws that entry in the graph. The graph draws every entry, and every link whose target the graph
holds. A panel on the right holds the selected entry in full. It carries the entry's fields, its
topics, the links it declares, the links reaching it, the identifiers it names that no file carries,
and its body.

One selection serves all three. Choosing in one place shows in the others, so a reader following a
link never loses the place they came from.

## What the graph draws

An entry is one shape, coloured by whether it decides or signals, greyed where the graph retired it,
and sized by how many links reach it. A link is one line, coloured by its kind, pointing at what it
names. A halo marks distance from the selection: red on the selection, purple and smaller one link
away, and purple and smaller again two links away. Colour alone would not survive a thousand entries
drawn at once, so size carries the same fact.

The camera flies to the selection, which a view of a whole graph draws far too small to find by eye.
With nothing selected the view frames the settled layout whole.

Repulsion reaches a bounded distance. Reaching further pushes an entry group linking to nothing else
far past every other, and a frame taking that in shrinks the body of the graph to a speck.

## What it costs

The simulation settles rather than running while a reader watches. A drawing that has come to rest
stays where it is until the selection moves. A body arrives as the tool wrote it, hard wrapped. The
panel rejoins a block of running prose, and leaves the lines of a block carrying a heading, a list, a
quotation, a table, or a fence. No view filters, so a reader meets every entry a graph holds.

## Rejected

Drawing one entry at a time loses. It shows nothing the table does not, and the relations are the
reason a graph exists.

Marking the selection by colour alone loses. A thousand shapes at once leave a single recoloured one
indistinguishable, and the halo carries the same fact at a size a reader finds.

Framing the whole graph whenever the layout settles loses. A reader arriving from the table sees the
entry they picked as one speck among a thousand.

Nothing follows from this on its own, which is what makes it terminal at capture. It fixes what the
two views show and commissions no further work. A change to either arrives as its own decision.
