Entry bodies render through `react-markdown` with `remark-gfm`, and no other Markdown parser enters.

An entry body is Markdown. It carries headings, lists, emphasis, code spans, fenced blocks,
quotations, tables, and links. A reader met all that as raw punctuation before, or as prose with its
wrapping guessed at.

The renderer builds elements rather than an HTML string. Markup written inside an entry is therefore
text a reader sees, never markup the page runs, and that holds without a sanitiser standing between
the two. Nothing in the viewer uses the escape hatch that writes raw HTML into a page. A link opens
away from the viewer.

## What it replaces

A hand-written splitter rejoined the wrapped lines of running prose and left every other block as
written. It never read a heading, a list, or a link. Two parsers disagreeing about one
body reads worse than either alone, so that splitter no longer exists.

## What it costs

Two packages rather than one, and a parser family larger than a minimal one. The whole bundle now
measures about 1.8 megabytes, though the 3D drawing library dominates that rather than this pair.

`marked` with `dompurify` loses. It parses to an HTML string, so the panel would need the escape
hatch. The body's safety would then rest on a cleaner's own settings, rather than on a parser
that cannot emit HTML at all. Growing the hand-written splitter loses too: tables and links would stay
unhandled, and every edge a real parser knows would arrive later as a defect a reader found.

Nothing follows from this on its own, which is what makes it terminal at capture.
