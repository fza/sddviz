Every entry record carries the links the entry's own file declares.

`Entries()` reports three further fields on each record. `Refs` lists what the frontmatter's `refs`
holds: for each link, the identifier it names, the kind of relation, and the sentence written about
it. `Closes` lists the identifiers of the signals the entry answers. `Supersedes` lists the
identifiers of the decisions it retires. The record reports all three as the file declares them.

A viewer that cannot show what points at what shows a list rather than a graph. The relations are the
reason a graph exists. Without them the 3D view has one drawn shape per entry and no line between any
two. A detail panel says nothing about where the entry it shows sits either.

One call keeps answering everything. The links travel on the record that already carries the entry.
The 3D view folds every record's links into one network, and the detail panel reads the selected
record's own links straight off it.

A second service method answering a flat list of source, identifier, and kind loses. It leaves the
record untouched. It also puts two shapes on the seam that have to move together. The detail panel
then joins that list back onto the record by hand to show what a link says.

Answering both loses for the same reason twice over. The same fact ships in two shapes that can
disagree, and every further kind of relation has to reach two places rather than one.

Two costs come with this. The front end drops a link naming an entry the graph does not hold. The
record reports what a file declares rather than what resolves against the rest of the graph. The
graph at `/Users/felix/Codepot/devops/format-d-fdbox` carries one such link among 2291. The three
frontmatter keys also join the ones this module already knows, so a schema that renames one costs the
reader a missing link rather than a refusal.

## What a record holds

- A record for an entry declaring `refs` carries one link per declared relation, in the order the
  file declares them, each with its identifier, its kind, and its description.
- A record for an entry declaring `closes` or `supersedes` carries those identifiers. A record for
  one declaring neither carries empty lists rather than a missing field.
- A record skips a malformed link, one that is not a mapping or that names no identifier, and keeps
  every other link that entry declares.

Nothing follows from this on its own, which is what makes it terminal at capture. It widens the shape
of a record that other entries commission work against, and it commissions none itself. A further
change to that shape arrives as its own decision.
